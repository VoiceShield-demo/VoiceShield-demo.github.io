VoiceShield: Practical and Perceptually Transparent Real-Time Voice Protection against AI Cloning

Anonymous authors

Abstract

Recent advances in AI voice cloning can reproduce a person's voice from only a few seconds of speech, enabling scalable impersonation, fraud, social engineering, and misinformation. Existing anti-cloning defenses, however, often rely on expensive per-utterance optimization and waveform-/spectrogram-level adversarial perturbations, making them difficult to scale or deploy in real time while potentially degrading perceptual and audio fidelity. In this paper, we present VoiceShield, a practical and perceptually transparent voice-protection system for both recorded and real-time speech. VoiceShield protects speech in the latent space of a pretrained generative speech model, leveraging its structural prior to produce speech-supported identity-disrupting modifications through a frozen decoder. Instead of optimizing each utterance separately, VoiceShield learns a feed-forward latent protector that generates these modifications for unseen speech in a single pass, with an identity-aware soft mask focusing them on speaker-relevant latent features. Building on this design, VoiceShield extends protection to continuously arriving speech through context-aware streaming, overlap-aware rendering, and a lightweight distilled vocoder. An audio-structure-preserving output mechanism further retains the original sampling rate and stereo structure. We evaluate VoiceShield against seven state-of-the-art defenses across ten recent open-source and commercial voice-cloning systems. VoiceShield achieves the strongest overall protection–quality trade-off at substantially lower protection cost than optimization-based defenses, as confirmed by objective metrics and a listening study. We further demonstrate real-time deployment across desktop, mobile, and edge devices.

Evaluation metric

About these samples

Every column contains the same source utterance and its protected outputs.

All files are mono, 16-bit PCM WAV recordings. Original and VoiceShield clips are 24 kHz; baseline outputs retain their method-produced native sampling rate and duration.

All seven baseline methods evaluated in the paper are included here.
